# usctts.com home, section by section, as a USC student

Caleb, 2026-10-05: "Put yourself in pov of a USC student at every section."
The student is a freshman or sophomore, a few weeks into the semester,
choosing which club gets their time. They've just seen TroyLabs, LavaLab and
CAIS++. The freshman review (scratchpad/freshman/freshman-review.md) found we
win "seems cool" and lose "want to join", because the home page talked to a
dentist. Each section below says what the student thinks today, what they need
from it, and the change.

## 1. The opening: the LA night grid, "USC's AI implementation lab."

- **Thinks:** "ok this is sick." Then, "what is it though?"
- **Needs:** in three seconds, what this club is to _them_.
- **Change:** keep the grid and the headline. Add one line in the readout that
  speaks to them: "Students who build real AI for real LA businesses." The
  rule against a lead paragraph stands, so the line lives in the mono status,
  in Caleb's voice.

## 2. The stream and the sunrise

- **Thinks:** "pretty," and scrolls faster.
- **Needs:** something to happen to them, not just to the map.
- **Change:** make this the "you" moment. Text the camera flies past: "This is
  you, a few weeks from now." The light leaving USC is a team of members
  heading out. Keep it short; it's a bridge.

## 3. Clay and Perplexity

- **Thinks:** "$20B, damn," then "BMA, who? What do I get?"
- **Needs:** what the partnership means for a member.
- **Change:** keep the logos and the count-up, and say what they get in one
  plain line: "You build on Clay and Perplexity, the same tools real GTM
  teams pay for, through our partner Blue Modern Advisory." The "as far as we
  know, the only university club" line stays small.

## 4. The deep dive (the machine film replaces the week panel)

- **Thinks today:** "why am I watching a dentist's calendar?"
- **Needs:** to see themselves doing the work, and that the work is cool.
- **Change:** the student is the main character. "Week 3. You and two friends
  take on a dental office in Koreatown." The machine film plays as the thing
  your team builds:

  - the city tray is the leads you find
  - the sorter is you deciding who's worth reaching
  - the typewriter is the replies your AI drafts
  - the mailbox is it going out
  - the block stack is the CRM growing

  Each stage's caption names the skill you learn there, in one short line
  that flies past (finding leads, writing prompts, wiring a CRM, teaching a
  client). The x-ray stays, so a curious student can drag and see the real
  table, the prompt and the manim clip. The business stays as the client, and
  the owner's version of this story lives on /work-with-us.

## 5. The ending: two doors on cardinal

- **Thinks today:** "applications open soon... so nothing to do."
- **Needs:** something to do today, and a reason to believe it's real.
- **Change:**
  - the student door reads "I'm at USC. Teach me to build that." and leads to
    the notify signup, so they leave their email right there, with no extra
    click
  - add the Instagram link next to it, so they can follow the club now
  - when there's a real info session or build night, its date and room go
    here, and nothing gets invented
  - keep the business door

## 6. Across every section

- **Phone first:** most students open this from an Instagram link on a phone,
  so every beat has to read at 390.
- **No WebGL fallback:** a static frame of the grid, so the first screens are
  never blank.
- **Words:** fewer per screen. A student skims.
- **Proof of people:** the moment there are real photos of a build night, they
  go in. Nothing beats real faces for "I want to join".
