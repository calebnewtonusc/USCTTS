## How both worlds fit

The light grid is the city, and the clay machine is what happens inside one
point of it. They meet in two places, and nowhere else.

- **Into the machine.** The camera dives toward the Koreatown point, and its
  glow fills the frame in the machine's ground colour (#F4EFE6). The film's
  first frames start defocused in that same colour, then the machine racks
  into focus. It's one move, a zoom through a point of light into a clay world.
- **Out of the machine.** The film's last frames pull back and up until the
  machine is small on its warm ground and the ground brightens to the same
  cream. The grid takes over behind it, with the machine now a single point
  of light again, and the rain fills the city cardinal into the ending.

Above the dive, everything is light (the future, Lemma). Inside, everything is
clay (the playground, Clay). The words are Caleb's voice the whole way
(young, Gavin).

## The page and every word

Rules:

- The student is the main character.
- The business is the client, and it's an example.
- Mono labels name the skill or the tool.
- At least one beat has no words.

1. **Opening (grid, night).**
   - H1: USC's AI implementation lab.
   - Status line: students who build real AI for real LA businesses
2. **Stream (grid, sunrise).** A flyby line: This is you, a few weeks from now.
3. **Partners (grid, sky).**
   - The logos, with $7.1B and $20B counting up and their "as of" dates.
   - Line: You build on Clay and Perplexity, the same tools real GTM teams pay
     for, through our partner Blue Modern Advisory.
   - Small: as far as we know, we're the only university club building on both
4. **The dive (grid into film).** Say your first project is a dental office in
   Koreatown, for example.
5. **The machine (film, scrubbed).** Each line rides one stage of the machine:
   - the city tray: First you find every business nearby that could use
     them. Label: finding leads, in Clay
   - the spiral and the gold sorter: Then you decide who's actually worth
     reaching, and teach the AI why. Label: qualifying, with Perplexity
     research
   - the typewriter: It drafts the first email. You fix it until it sounds
     like a person. Label: prompting
   - the mailbox: no words; the flag pops
   - the block stack: Every reply lands in a CRM you wired up, so nothing gets
     lost. Label: the CRM
   - the pull-back: Then you teach the office to run it without you.
6. **Out (film into grid, rain to cardinal).**
7. **Ending (cardinal).**
   - Student door: I'm at USC. Teach me to build that. Then the email signup
     inline, with "applications open soon", and the Instagram link.
   - Business door: I run a business. Book 30 minutes with Caleb.
   - The footer folds in below.

The x-ray stays on the machine. Dragging it over a stage shows that stage's
real artifact: the Clay lead table, the qualifying prompt, the drafted email
and its prompt, the CRM workflow, the lesson outline. Each comes with its
manim clip.

## What this script doesn't claim

Every client and number in the story is an example and labelled as one.
There's no "week 3", no team size, and no member count. The partnership line
keeps "through our partner Blue Modern Advisory".
