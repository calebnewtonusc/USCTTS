# Two sites, one team

Written 2026-09-22 from Caleb's own words in the Tyler strategy call and the
September outreach. Every claim below traces to something on disk; anything that
does not is marked `NEED:` and must not ship.

## The split

**TTS** is the USC-facing org: applied AI implementation, recruiting, teaching.
Its reader is a USC student deciding which club to put a semester into.

**T Combinator** is the founder-facing branch: USC builders taking real
ownership of a role at a YC company, free, cohort capped at three.
Its reader is **a YC founder who just replied "more info" to a cold DM.**

That second one is the sharper brief, because the page has exactly one job.
The DM already worked: [outreach/00-THE-MESSAGE.md](../../t-combinator/outreach/00-THE-MESSAGE.md)
records that most recipients **read it without replying**, which is a reply
problem and not a hook problem. The site is what a founder hits when they are
60/40, and it has to close the gap between interested and on a call.

For USC's purposes T Combinator sits inside TTS. Same team, same advisors, same
alumni. One roster file, [data/people.ts](../data/people.ts), feeds both.

## What TTS actually is

Caleb's framing, 2026-09-22: USC's only applied AI implementation club that does
real work for companies, across engineering and GTM engineering, and teaches
people to finesse for companies, for their own lives, and anything in between.

The current site says something else entirely. It says "student-run tech
consulting org," "completely pro bono," "No experience required. No
gatekeeping." That is the positioning being replaced, not a starting point.

## What we attack, and what we do not

Caleb asked for the sites to dog on the consulting clubs. The attack lands on
**the format**, never on a named USC organization:

- A deck is the deliverable, and most of the time nobody implements it.
- Members present recommendations and leave before anything ships.
- Nobody touches a tool the client keeps using after the semester ends.

That is Caleb's own critique, verbatim: *"that's all these BTG companies. A lot
of time it's not even implemented. It's just making a deck."*

Naming TroyLabs or ProductSC on a public page is a different move with real
cost, because TTS is mid-push to become an official RSO. The house rule already
exists in [pro-bono-post](https://github.com/calebnewtonusc/pro-bono-post):
**punch at the experience, never at the people.** It applies here.

## The proof we are allowed to use

There is **no client logo wall**, on either site.
[CLIENTS.md](../../t-combinator/CLIENTS.md) records zero signed engagements in
the Praxis vertical and seven YC clients that are written down nowhere. Until
that list exists, no company name goes on a public page.

What is real and sourced:

1. **The alumni bench.** Fifteen people who started in this club: Apple,
   Palantir, Bloomberg twice, Reddit, Capital One, NBC Universal, Fastly, Epic,
   PwC, Citi, Jefferies, Nomura. Plus advisors at Google, McKinsey, Reddit and
   the founder of Mixbook. The outreach line "our people are at Google, Apple,
   McKinsey and Reddit" is fully sourced from this, and it undersells it.
2. **The artifact.** Built 2026-09-15 for the Praxis portfolio: 180 companies
   enumerated, 388 roles pulled, 2,158 LinkedIn connections matched to them, and
   a data-quality trap caught before it put a fabricated number at the top of
   the list. Demonstrably done, not claimed.
3. **Dormant to a real roster in three months**, with zero members inherited.
4. **What we turn down.** Clinical work, on-site work, hard engineering, and
   anything where the first status update reveals the team cannot do it.
   Saying no in public is the cheapest credibility on either page.

Leading with the artifact instead of with logos is not a workaround. A founder
who has been pitched by four student clubs has seen the logos and has never seen
the thing.
