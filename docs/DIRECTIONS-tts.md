# Three directions for TTS

Reader: a USC student with four club tabs open, deciding which one gets their
semester. Every other tab says "build", "impact" and "community". The one this
reader trusts fastest is the one that shows them the work instead of saying
the word.

## A. The lab report

**Stance.** The page reads like a short report from a lab that ships: one
centred column of prose at reading width, broken by wide exhibits that are
not pictures of work but the work itself. The main exhibit is a real
pipeline executing on its real dataset in the visitor's browser.

**Refuses.** Monospace anywhere, because the USC dark-terminal look belongs
to four other clubs; any number the page did not compute or `POSITIONING.md`
does not source; highlighter fills (T Combinator's device); italic accent
phrases and uppercase kickers, the median generated "tasteful" page; cards;
and a second colour.

**Type.** Source Serif 4 carries everything a person reads, headings
included, at a large optical size and weight 600 for display. Schibsted
Grotesk is the instrument face: labels, buttons, captions and every figure,
with tabular numerals doing the job mono usually does.

**Colour.** Warm paper and warm ink, plus one vermilion, used the way a lab
uses red ink: it marks a value that was computed in your browser just now,
and the kept or set-aside stamp the pipeline puts on a record. Static claims
never get it.

**Signature.** The run: 388 real job postings from a venture fund's portfolio board,
taken through five stages live, with measured milliseconds per stage and a
switch that skips the verification step so the reader watches a fake number
take first place.

**Motion.** Mechanical, like a press. Counters step, stamps land, lines draw.
Linear and `steps()` easing, no springs, no float.

## B. The campus poster

**Stance.** Loud, printed, physical. Risograph two-colour, halftone portraits
of the real alumni, oversized condensed headlines that run off the edge like a
flyer stapled to a board outside Leavey.

**Refuses.** Screens of product UI, dark mode, subtle anything, more than two
inks, rectangles that do not touch an edge.

**Type.** A heavy condensed grotesk for headlines, a typewriter-ish body.

**Colour.** Two spot inks overprinting on newsprint, with misregistration as
texture.

**Signature.** The alumni bench as a printed roster wall that peels page by
page on scroll.

**Motion.** Paper physics: slight rotation, slap-down entrances, ink bleed.

## C. The open notebook

**Stance.** The club as a public lab notebook. Dated entries, newest first,
each one a thing tried, with what broke. Recruiting happens in the margin.

**Refuses.** A hero, a CTA above the fold, marketing language, images that
are not screenshots of real work.

**Type.** A humanist sans for entries, handwritten-style annotations in the
margins.

**Colour.** Graph paper, pencil grey, one red for corrections.

**Signature.** Entries that expand into the actual artifact inline.

**Motion.** Almost none; a margin note writes itself in once.

## The choice: A, the lab report

This reader has seen a dozen club pages say "you will build real things", so
the fastest trust comes from watching a real thing run on real data while
they read, which only A puts at the centre. B recruits on vibe, which is the
exact thing the genre research says loses to work, and C needs a dozen dated
entries that do not exist yet, so it would ship looking empty.

## Kept apart from T Combinator

T Combinator chose a dark scoping sheet: one sans family (Archivo), left
aligned rows with margin labels, a lime highlighter that means "read from your
listing". TTS is the other side of every one of those choices: light paper,
serif-led, a centred essay column with wide exhibits and no margin labels, and
an accent that is ink colour, not a fill. Its signature runs on a dataset;
T Combinator's runs on one founder's company.
