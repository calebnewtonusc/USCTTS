# Craft rules for T Combinator

Researched 2026-10-03 before any code. The genre is a page that converts a
skeptical founder: an accelerator's own terms page, an investor's program page,
and the personalized landing page an outbound sequence links to. Rules, each
with its source. The reader is a YC founder who replied "more info" to a cold
DM and is 60/40 on a twenty-minute call (docs/POSITIONING.md).

## Sources read

- **Y Combinator, "The YC Deal"** (ycombinator.com/deal). The plainest terms
  page in the genre.
- **a16z speedrun** (speedrun.a16z.com). Program page aimed at founders.
- **South Park Commons, F26 Founder Fellowship**
  (southparkcommons.com/news/f26-founder-fellowship).
- **Prismic, "How to build high-converting ABM landing pages"**, a
  practitioner guide to pages built for one named account.
- **Harry Dry, Marketing Examples**: the three copy tests (visualise, falsify,
  nobody else can say it), via his "How I Write" interview.
- **Peep Laja (Wynter)**: "Level 0" of B2B messaging is clarity and relevance:
  what is it, what is it for, is it a fit for me.
- Repo sources: `t-combinator/outreach/00-THE-MESSAGE.md`, the honest-read
  table in particular, and `t-combinator/OFFER.md`.

## Rules

1. **Terms first, in one breath, then the mechanics.** YC's page opens on the
   amount and the stake, then says the money is "not contingent on hitting any
   milestones", and only then explains the math. Our equivalent: free, three
   companies, one call to hand it off, kill it whenever. Above the fold. (YC deal)
2. **Pre-empt the "gotcha" out loud.** YC writes "we try hard to avoid any
   'gotcha' terms". A founder reading a free offer from students assumes a
   catch, so the page names why it is free in the founder's terms: our people
   want a YC company on their resume. (YC deal; honest-read table in 00-THE-MESSAGE)
3. **Proof is a named person, not a logo.** SPC lists ten investors by name
   with links; speedrun names its founders. We have no clients, so the named
   people are the bench and its companies, and no client name appears.
   (SPC; speedrun; DESIGN.md rule 13)
4. **One relevant detail beats ten irrelevant ones.** The ABM guide's test for
   every personalized element: "does this detail help them?" Show the
   founder's data only where it changes what we would do. Never display data
   for the sake of showing we have it. (Prismic)
5. **Personalize the headline and the proof, not the decoration.** Headline,
   value line and the matched work are the fields worth rewriting per
   account. (Prismic)
6. **A missing field falls back to the segment, never to an error.** Prismic
   does not cover it, so this is ours: no headcount means no headcount line,
   and an unreachable data source still renders the brief from the index.
7. **One CTA per page, matched to stage.** A late-stage reader gets a meeting
   ask; the guide says to remove competing navigation. Ours is the twenty
   minutes. Everything else on the page is a link to read more, not a second
   ask. (Prismic)
8. **The ask costs nothing to answer.** The DM's read-without-reply problem was
   the closing "Down?", a commitment. The fix was a question answerable in a
   sentence. The page's form asks that same question. (00-THE-MESSAGE)
9. **Say who it is NOT for.** SPC explicitly refuses ideas, timelines and
   hustle framing; that makes the yes believable. Our four refusals are
   published, and on a personalized page they are evaluated against the
   founder's own listing. (SPC; OFFER.md)
10. **Every claim falsifiable, visualisable, and ours.** Dry's three tests,
    applied to every sentence. "Hand off the thing you keep not doing" passes
    the first. "Innovative talent" fails all three. (Dry)
11. **Clarity before persuasion.** What is it, what is it for, is it a fit for
    me, answered before any proof. (Laja)
12. **Forms: three fields or fewer, 44px targets, under 3s on 4G.** (Prismic)
13. **Urgency only if it is true.** SPC repeats a real deadline; speedrun a
    real demo day. We have no published date, so the page carries none rather
    than a fake countdown. [NEED: a spring cohort decision date]
